# LebanVolt-composite-material-
## Project objectives 
With the grande objective of designing a wrist mounted hybrid PENG TENG, I built off my work in piezoelectric polymers, conducting background research in bio-compatible piezoelectric materials. I have concluded that a glycin, glycerol alginate composite is the best way forward- allowing for ease of manufacturability without comprimising voltage output per unit of strain (piezoelectric coeffecient).

## Methodology 


1) 1.5 wt % chitosan solution was prepared by mixing chitosan powder with a 1% v/v acetic acid aqueous solution. 


2) The following glycine to chitosan ratios of 0.4:1, 0.8:1, 1.2:1, and 2.7:1, will be prepared by dissolving diﬀerent amounts of glycine powder in the prepared 1.5 wt % chitosan solution by stirring. 


3) The well-mixed glycine/chitosan solution will be drop casted inside a polystyrene Petri dish, followed by drying at room temperature for 24−48 h. 


4) Solid core insulated copper wires will be soldered to 1 cm x 1cm pieces of copper tape. Aluminum tape electrodes will be stuck against either glycine alginate glycine composite. The copper tape copper wire electrode extension will be stuck onto both aluminum tape electrodes. 


5) The film composite was cut into a auxetic honeycomb pattern using the bambu lab H2D plotter extension  


## Why? 
Energy has been, and will continue to be, critical for economic, social and technological growth. The unfortunate truth, however, is that an estimated 730 million people, as of 2025, lacked access to electricity.  While leveraging the continued decrease in the price of renewable energy may be a solution, infrastructure and zoning challenges remain  an  obstacle for the widespread adoption of renewables. Additionally,  renewables that depend on atmospheric conditions face a limiting factor as meteorological conditions vary. 
Electricity produced by human biomechanical motion, using mechanical energy nanogenerators (MENGs), include triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs) and piezoelectric nannogenerators (PENGs).  While such technologies prove themselves advantageous, by harnessing  human biomechanical motion – a ubiquitous source of electricity – they face their own set of challenges, including difficulties in manufacturing, , dependence on brittle and toxic materials, and scaling difficulties. To address these challenges,  I conceived LebanVolt a wearable polymer-based piezoelectric-triboelectric hybrid nanogenerator, with the objective of creating  an economical, scalable device that transforms the motion of the wrist and upper forearm into usable power. 

While past prototypes of LebanVolt (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wxHNFwfxpGI&list=PL4c0XTaN5QL7R7dcJk9QdU_dNLjhbm2Hm) have depended on outsourcing non biocompatible toxic piezoelectric materials, LebanVolt v2 aims to synthesize a biodegradable, economical, highly efficient piezoelectric polymer. I have concluded that a glycine, glycerol alginate composite is the best way forward- allowing for ease of manufacturability without compromising voltage output per unit of strain (piezoelectric coefficient).
